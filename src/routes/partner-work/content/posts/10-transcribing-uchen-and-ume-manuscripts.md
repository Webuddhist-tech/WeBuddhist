# Typing the hardest pages: Uchen and Ume transcription

_Part of the BDRC E-Text Corpus project, a collaboration between BDRC and WeBuddhist (Dharmaduta Services LLP), supported by the Khyentse Foundation. [All posts →](00-index.md)_

---

Machine learning can only learn what people can show it. For the pages where OCR struggled most (rare scripts, manuscripts full of abbreviations), our teams typed out the text from scratch, character by character, using tools we built and refined over three versions.

## What we delivered

**Transcriptions.** After the benchmark pages, annotators transcribed **2,050 Uchen pages**, selected one per volume so every page is a different style, and **5,081 Ume pages** to fill the gaps identified in OCR analysis.

**Three generations of tools.** Our first tool used a five-role workflow inspired by the ACIP project. **Image Transcription v2** has two annotators transcribe each page independently, two reviewers compare and fix, and a final reviewer locks in the approved text, while tracking each person's contribution and rejection rate. **v3** tests a simpler flow: three annotators and one reviewer.

![Transcription tool in use](images/p4-transcription-tool-ume.jpg)

![Review step](images/p4-transcription-tool-review.jpg)

**A measured comparison.** We ran both v2 and v3 on the same 385 pages:

![Workflow comparison](images/chart-p5-itv2-vs-itv3.png)

v2 was more accurate (annotation error 3.77% vs. 4.87%, annotator agreement 0.939 vs. 0.922) and had half the rejection rate; cost per page was nearly equal. v3 was simpler, which reviewers liked. The full report is on the OpenPecha forum.

## Challenges, and how we overcame them

**First-time online workers.** Several annotators, including Bönpo Kushos recruited for their skill in reading Kungyig cursive, were new to digital forms and video calls. We provided one-to-one laptop training and handled account and banking setup for each.

**Quality in a hard script.** We screened candidates in Kungyig reading and ran daily checks.

**Choosing which pages to type.** With no obvious best selection, we picked images balanced across scripts and adjusted batches as models improved.

## Why it matters

Hand-typed pages are a small but precious resource: diverse, carefully checked and aimed at exactly the places where models fail.
