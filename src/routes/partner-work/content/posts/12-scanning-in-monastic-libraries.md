# Scanning the shelves: digitizing modern Tibetan books in India

_Part of the BDRC E-Text Corpus project, a collaboration between BDRC and WeBuddhist (Dharmaduta Services LLP), supported by the Khyentse Foundation. [All posts →](00-index.md)_

---

Modern printed books give the cleanest OCR, but many aren't in any digital archive. Our field teams worked inside monastic libraries to scan prioritized volumes, choosing, with BDRC, books that don't exist online yet.

## What we delivered

- **Catalogues** of four libraries (Gyuto, Sherab Ling, Sherab Ling University and Dzongsar, over 25,000 volumes), pre-selected by us and prioritized by BDRC as "not wanted", "low priority" or "high priority".
- **Scans** of 1,033 volumes from three libraries, plus **493 high-cost volumes** (300 on Gesar and 193 on the five lesser sciences) that were previously scanned at Sherab Ling.
- All 1,500 volumes catalogued by BDRC for publication on its website, with images flowing into the OCR and cataloging workflow.

## Challenges, and how we overcame them

**Slow machines.** Page-turning scanners needed constant glass cleaning, and some were unstable (colour/DPI settings, worn roller rubbers). The team adapted workflows and kept consistent weekly output.

**Remote sites.** Internet was patchy and power cuts frequent, so we processed offline and uploaded overnight.

**Distance and weather.** Annotators at Sherab Ling commuted about an hour each way, and monsoon season brought more disruptions.

**Permissions.** Formal permissions at some libraries took time, and teams reorganized and resumed once they came through.

**Unpredictable books.** Quality depends on each book's size and condition, so batches were checked as they went.

## Why it matters

Each scanned volume is a modern, high-quality source that can anchor a text in the final corpus, and an addition to the open archive.
