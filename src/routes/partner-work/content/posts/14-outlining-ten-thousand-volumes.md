# Ten thousand volumes, text by text: the outlining and cataloging operation

_Part of the BDRC E-Text Corpus project, a collaboration between BDRC and WeBuddhist (Dharmaduta Services LLP), supported by the Khyentse Foundation. [All posts →](00-index.md)_

---

Knowing which volumes exist isn't enough. The goal of this deliverable: find **every individual text** inside every volume, with its title and author. It is the most people-intensive part of the whole project.

## At a glance

|                                                |                                                                                                            |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Volumes outlined                               | **10,000**                                                                                                 |
| Individual texts identified                    | **186,300** with title and author                                                                          |
| Team                                           | **20** annotators (9 full-time, 11 part-time) in the first two months, then **30 FTE** with 3 coordinators |
| BDRC work/author IDs linked in the pilot phase | **2,001** (from 907 volumes and 10,838 works)                                                              |

## How it works

Annotators label every text's title and author **exactly as they appear** in the source, without editing or completing them, to avoid introducing human error. Volumes are prioritized by quality: modern publications and gold-standard e-texts first, then clear blockprints. Reviewers and automated matching handle links to BDRC IDs.

## Challenges, and how we overcame them

**Duplicate records.** When annotators were asked to link to BDRC's database during outlining, many duplicate IDs were created. We **split the workflow in two**: segmentation and title/author labelling first, ID linking by reviewers second.

**Spelling variations.** Titles and authors appear in many spellings. Annotators referred to BDRC's database, and the team kept coordinators close for quick decisions.

**Messy source volumes.** In some batches entries were out of order, lacked source references or had incomplete titles. We reconstructed titles from BDRC's database, held frequent check-ins with BDRC and the developers, and added a final spot-check round.

**Treasure texts.** Terma titles can be ambiguous, and treasure revealers are not the original authors. We agreed a protocol with BDRC for how to label them.

**Moving workflow.** As KPIs were still being clarified, annotators trained while the interface was upgraded. Close facilitation by coordinators carried the team through.

## Why it matters

This catalog is what lets the project recognize the same text in different editions and choose the best version, and what makes the final corpus findable by title and author.
